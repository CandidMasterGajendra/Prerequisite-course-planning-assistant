# Catalog-Grounded Prerequisite & Course Planning Assistant

**Scope and data.** This project is a bounded advising assistant for Oregon State University's 2026–27 Computer Science major and Computer Systems option. The curated corpus contains 48 official course-specific pages, two program pages, and two policy pages (52 distinct URLs, 13,473 focused words), meeting the assessment's 25+ document/page route. Every source records its URL, access date, catalog year, and content hash; `docs/sources.md` describes coverage.

**Architecture.** A LangGraph workflow runs five inspectable stages: intake, retrieval, deterministic rule/planning logic, optional GPT wording, and verification. The local RAG index contains 99 passages embedded with `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5` (384 dimensions). Course descriptions remain intact; long program and policy pages use 230-word chunks with 35-word overlap. Retrieval uses normalized dot-product similarity, top-k=5, exact course-code boosts, and metadata boosts for program and policy queries. This hybrid design preserves exact identifiers while supporting natural-language questions such as “Database Systems.”

**Reasoning and safety.** Twenty-seven manually reviewed prerequisite expressions support AND/OR groups, minimum grades, placement alternatives, concurrent prerequisites, and three-valued results: `eligible`, `not_eligible`, or `needs_more_info`. Five ambiguously worded rules, including CS 325, deliberately abstain. The planner proposes only reviewed required courses whose prerequisites pass, stays within the requested credit limit, and treats term offering, seats, timetable conflicts, and transfer approval as unknown. Deterministic program answers cover degree/option totals and elective constraints. Every response follows the required Answer/Plan, Why, Citations, Clarifying Questions, and Assumptions/Not in Catalog structure, with an additional Next Steps section.

**Generation and verification.** GPT is limited to concise wording and defaults to the low-cost `gpt-5-mini`; it cannot select courses or decide eligibility. The app runs fully without GPT. The verifier validates passage IDs, URLs, headings, target-course evidence, multi-course chain evidence, program membership, credit limits, decision consistency, and course scope. Unsupported drafts fall back to deterministic wording. A live smoke test exposed an overly strict prerequisite-code check; the fix is protected by a regression test.

**Evaluation.** The required 25-query set contains 10 direct prerequisite checks, five chain questions with evidence from at least two course pages, five program-requirement questions, and five not-in-catalog/trick questions. Two extra plan cases bring the total to 27. Development results are: eligibility correctness 15/15, abstention accuracy 5/5, citation coverage on catalog-claim responses 22/22, valid chain evidence 5/5, and plan-credit validity 2/2. Twelve tuned retrieval cases score recall@5 of 1.00 and MRR@5 of 0.958. These are regression results, not independent real-world accuracy claims.

**Next improvements.** Expand reviewed rules for course-specific corequisites, repeats, transfer equivalencies, residency, and all elective constraints; resolve ambiguous catalog wording through an authoritative source; freeze and independently label a held-out set; obtain human sentence-level citation review; compare the custom vector index with FAISS/Chroma and lexical baselines; then deploy the local web demo and record a short walkthrough.
